# Visual noise

Supporting material for [`philosophy.md`](../philosophy/philosophy.md) §10 (Attention), §8.7 (Restraint), and §11 (Visual distinctions should mean something). It adds depth, not rules.

**Core question (§10):** what deserves attention right now, and what can afford to be quiet?

Covers excessive borders, unnecessary badges, many small competing signals, and asymmetry without purpose.

## Recognising it

- Borders around every cell, field group, and section, plus dividers between them.
- Badges, counts, and "new" labels on many items at once.
- Many small emphasised things (bold labels, icons, coloured text, tags) at the same visual weight.
- Elements slightly out of alignment, or asymmetric layouts that don't lead anywhere.
- No clear focal point: the eye wanders.

## Why it happens

Each added signal is a local fix ("make this visible", "separate this"), made without looking at the whole screen. Noise is the sum of individually reasonable decisions.

## Why it matters

- **Attention.** Every border, badge, and emphasis spends from a finite budget (§10). When the budget is spread evenly, nothing stands out.
- **Clarity.** People must process more to find what matters (§8.2).
- **Alerts lose force.** When many things badge for attention, the one that matters is missed.

## When it is not a problem

- Dense expert interfaces can carry many signals if they are consistent and hierarchical (§8.12).
- Borders that express structure: tables, input fields, genuinely separate regions.
- Badges on the few items that need attention now.
- Deliberate asymmetry that guides reading order or expresses character.

## Diagnosing

- List the five loudest elements on the screen. Do they match the five most important?
- For each border or divider, does spacing alone already express the grouping?
- For each badge, does the user need to act on it now?
- Is misalignment intentional, and does it lead the eye somewhere?

## Repair

1. Quiet the competitors rather than amplifying the target.
2. Replace borders with spacing or surface change where grouping is already clear.
3. Keep badges for counts and statuses that need attention; remove the rest.
4. Align to a shared structure; keep asymmetry only where it has a purpose.

## Overcorrection

Removing all separation can make grouping ambiguous and dense data harder to scan. Reduce noise, not structure.
